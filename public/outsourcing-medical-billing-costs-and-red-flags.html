Outsourcing medical billing: how it is priced and what to watch for

Outsourcing can free up your team, but only if you pick the right partner. Here is what to ask.

Request a consultationHow we work

By Arovia RCM · Published 2026-10-25

How billing companies usually charge

  • A percentage of collections. The fee moves with what you collect.
  • A flat monthly fee. Easy to plan, and it should match the scope of work.
  • A fee per claim or per hour. Common for specific tasks such as follow-up.

Prices vary by specialty, volume and scope, so ask for the model in writing and check what is included.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Will you sign a Business Associate Agreement before seeing patient information?
  • Who does the work, and how are they trained and screened?
  • What do I receive each week, and can I see the same numbers myself?
  • Do I keep access to my data, payer portals and claims if I leave?
  • Is there a pilot period, and how do we measure it?
  • What is the notice period if I want to stop?

Red flags

  • Promises of guaranteed collections or a fixed increase in revenue.
  • No written agreement or no Business Associate Agreement.
  • Reports that are vague or only shared on request.
  • Long lock-in contracts with no pilot.
  • The company controls your payer portals or bank account in a way you cannot undo.
